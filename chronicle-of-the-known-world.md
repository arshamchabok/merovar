happens to own some rocks.

# BOOK ONE: MEROVAR

**7,435,000 souls · 1,481,600 swords · Three crowns · 61 holdings**

They will tell you in Crownford that Merovar was always Merovar. The records in Travos say
otherwise, and the records in Travos are eleven hundred years old and have never been
edited. Merovar was a Kadreshi holding. Ships came west, men got off them, and four
centuries later their grandchildren declared that they had been here since the world was
made.

Kadresh has never conceded the point. In every letter their scribes still write *the
western holdings*, and in every reply Crownford's scribes strike the phrase out. This has
been going on for six hundred years. Both sides consider it a form of diplomacy.

Three crowns share the continent. **Crownvale** holds the lakes and the money.
**Sablereach** holds the volcanic south and very nearly a million swords. **Norwood** holds
the cold, and holds it well, and has never been taken. No one crown can claim the
overlordship alone. Every century or so, two of them come to an understanding, and the
third has a very bad decade indeed.

---

## THE KINGDOM OF CROWNVALE

### House Brenn · Seat at Crownford · 2,883,000 souls · 309,900 swords · 19 holdings

Crownvale does not conquer. It is important to understand this, because it is not the same
as saying Crownvale is peaceful. Crownvale *purchases*. It marries. It advances a loan at
a generous rate to a neighbour in a difficult season, and it is still holding the paper on
that loan when the neighbour's grandson comes to court to swear fealty, and everyone
present agrees it was a most amicable arrangement.

The realm sits in the lake basin at the heart of Merovar, and every road that matters runs
through it. That is not an accident of geography. Four Brenn kings spent their reigns
buying the land the roads would need.

---

### CROWNFORD — the White City

**House Brenn · 1,500,000 souls · 130,000 swords**

One soul in every twelve alive lives inside these walls.

Crownford is built in white limestone on the northern shore of the great lake, and on a
clear morning it can be seen from Goldspire, nine leagues distant, glowing like something
left out in the sun. The walls are eleven miles around. No army has ever tested them. Three
separate wars have ended in the season *before* the march on Crownford, when a general sat
down with a quartermaster and did the arithmetic of feeding a siege through a Merovaran
winter.

The crown itself is worn reversed. The story is this: the second Brenn king, Aemon called
the Patient, did not take the crown from his dying father. He took it from his elder
brother, who was wearing it at the time, and who did not survive the taking. When the
smiths asked how he wished it set upon his brow, he told them to set it upside down, so
that every man who looked at him would remember exactly how he had come by it.

Eleven Brenn kings since have worn it the same way. Not one has explained why to a
foreigner.

**Words:** *All Rivers Run Here*
**Banner:** A silver crown reversed upon deep green.

---

### RIVERMOND — the Confluence

**House Alchem · 500,000 souls · 30,000 swords**

Where the Duskwater meets the Bellrun, and every barge in the kingdom must choose a side of
the island or pay to pass between.

House Alchem were not lords. They were apothecaries, and before that they were
money-changers, and the older houses of Crownvale have been finding graceful ways to
mention this for two hundred and forty years. The Alchems bear it with a patience that
their rivals find more insulting than any retort. When Lord Perrin Alchem was told at a
Crownford feast that his grandmother had smelled of vinegar, he agreed, remarked that she
had also died the richest woman in the realm, and asked whether more wine could be brought.

They deal in transformation — of copper into coin, of debt into land, of a merchant family
into a great house. Their words are a joke that stopped being a joke about a century ago.

**Words:** *We Turn Base Things*
**Banner:** A gold retort upon black.

---

### The Castles of Crownvale

**KINGSWARD** — *House Kenward* — 60,000 souls, 16,000 swords
A *ward* is a guardianship, and the Kenwards were the king's ward before they were lords of
one. The first of them was a boy of nine taken as a hostage after a rebellion that his
father lost. He was raised in Crownford alongside the prince, and when the prince became
king, he gave the boy a castle and the duty of standing between the throne and anyone who
approached it. Six centuries on, no Kenward has ever failed in that duty, and the family
still sends its second sons to Crownford at nine years old. They are no longer hostages.
Nobody has thought to stop the custom.
**Words:** *We Stand Before the Throne* · **Banner:** A silver shield upon green.

**GOLDSPIRE** — *House Dayne* — 55,000 souls, 19,000 swords
The tower is gilded to its cap and can be seen from the capital. This has bankrupted House
Dayne twice. On both occasions the family sold land, sold horses, sold plate, and regilded
it. When the present lord's grandfather was asked by a Brenn king whether the expense might
not be better spent on his garrison, he replied that any fool could buy soldiers, but only
a Dayne could be seen from Crownford. He then borrowed the money from House Alchem at a
rate that his descendants are still paying.
**Words:** *Let Them See Us* · **Banner:** A gold spire upon white.

**LAKEGUARD** — *House Meryne* — 50,000 souls, 17,000 swords
The water approach to the capital passes beneath Lakeguard's walls, and the Merynes have
held those walls for nine generations. They have also married into House Brenn four times,
which means the current king is a Meryne on his mother's side and everyone at court has
agreed not to find this significant. The family's watchword is not a boast about vigilance.
It is a reminder to the crown that the watching goes both ways.
**Words:** *The Water Is Watched* · **Banner:** A silver eye upon blue.

**BLACKBRIDGE** — *House Crosswell* — 45,000 souls, 14,000 swords
The bridge is basalt. There is no basalt in Crownvale — it comes from Sablereach, five
hundred miles south, and the cost of hauling it must have been ruinous. The Crosswells have
held the crossing for three hundred years and have never given an account of why the first
of them insisted on southern stone. The Sablereach lords know. They have never said either.
**Words:** *We Bear the Weight* · **Banner:** A black span upon grey.

**ROSEFAST** — *House Roselyn* — 41,000 souls, 9,500 swords
The most beautiful castle in Merovar and among the least defensible: low walls, wide
windows, a water garden where a moat should be. It has fallen twice. Both times it was
given back — once by a Sablereach general who said he would not be the man remembered for
ruining it, and once by a Norwood raiding lord who liked the gardens and did not wish to
winter in them. The Roselyns treat this as proof of a strategy rather than an accident of
taste.
**Words:** *Held Gently* · **Banner:** A white rose upon red.

**FAIRKEEP** — *House Fairbourne* — 36,000 souls, 10,000 swords
Every land dispute in Crownvale is heard at Fairkeep, and has been since the crown granted
the right nine generations ago in place of a debt it could not pay. The Fairbournes are
poorer in swords than any house of their rank and richer in obligation than all of them
together. A Fairbourne judgement has never been overturned. Twice it has been ignored, and
on both occasions the ignoring house found that no other lord in the realm would treat with
it until the matter was settled.
**Words:** *Fairly, and Finally* · **Banner:** Gold scales upon white.

**PRIDEFORD** — *House Lionheart* — 15,000 souls, 3,800 swords
The smallest castle in Crownvale bearing the largest name in it. The Lionhearts hold a
river ford and a reputation, and the reputation is four hundred years older than their
claim to the ford. They fight in every war, on whichever side has asked first, and they
have never once been on the winning side two wars running. The house is beloved throughout
Merovar and taken seriously nowhere.
**Words:** *Pride Is Not a Sin* · **Banner:** A gold lion passant upon crimson.

**Of Silverpool and House Silvertongue.** The name was an insult. A Crownvale chancellor
used it of the family's founder in open court, meaning that the man could not be trusted to
say a true thing plainly. The founder thanked him for it, adopted it within the year, and
spent the remainder of his life negotiating treaties on the crown's behalf. Five of those
treaties still hold.
**Words:** *We Have Said Worse*

**Of Coin Market and House Forage.** Fifteen thousand souls and the busiest square in the
realm. House Forage charge nothing whatsoever to trade at Coin Market and take a tenth of
the value of every dispute settled there — and where merchants gather, disputes are the one
crop that never fails. They have grown quietly rich on other men's disagreements and are
scrupulously fair, because a reputation for fairness is what brings the disagreements in.
**Words:** *Bring It Here*

## SABLEREACH

### House Drago · Seat at Dragonhill · 3,007,000 souls · 964,000 swords · 24 holdings

Here is the fact that governs the politics of the world, and it is a fact, not a rumour:
**Sablereach fields nine hundred and sixty-four thousand men.** Crownvale fields three
hundred and ten. Norwood two hundred and eight. The southern kingdom could take the
continent in a single campaigning season, and every soldier in it knows this, and every
king in Crownford has known it for four hundred years.

It has never marched.

There are as many explanations as there are maesters willing to be paid for one. That the
Dragos are bound by an oath older than the crown they might take. That the army is not for
Merovar at all and never was. That the volcanic soil of Sablereach grows grapes and grain
in such quantity that a southern lord has simply never wanted anything badly enough to
march for it. The Sablereach lords are asked this at every feast in Crownford, and they
smile, and they refill the asker's cup, and they change the subject to the vintage.

The land itself is black — old lava fields gone to soil, hot springs, vineyards climbing
ash slopes, and stone the colour of a closed eye. Everything here is built from it.

---

### SUNSCAR — the City at the Wound

**House Hutton · 1,100,000 souls · 125,000 swords**

Behind the city, the plain is split by a fissure eleven miles long that glows dull red on
cold nights. It has been called the Scar since before there was a city, and it has never
closed.

The first Hutton built here because the ground was warm and nothing would grow anywhere
else that winter. His neighbours told him he was building beside a wound in the world. He
told them a wound is only dangerous if it is fresh, and this one was old, and old wounds
are simply the shape a thing has decided to be.

Eleven centuries later SunScar is the second city of Merovar and the Huttons are the
wealthiest house in the south, and the rest of the continent still considers the whole
enterprise deeply characteristic of Sablereach.

**Words:** *Scarred, Not Slain*
**Banner:** A red fissure upon black.

---

### BRINZE PORN — the Standing Army

**House Tharryan · 900,000 souls · 650,000 swords**

Seven people in every ten who live here carry a spear.

There is no other place in the world remotely like this. Brinze Porn alone fields more men
than the Aldermarch, the Silver Isles, and the whole of Amberfold combined. It is not a
city with a garrison. It is an army that has grown a city around itself the way a scar
grows over a blade, and the civilian trades — the bakers, the smiths, the tanners, the
rope-walks — exist very largely to keep the army fed and shod and armed.

The Tharryans have held it for six hundred years. They have never marched it anywhere.
They drill it. Every spring the whole host musters on the black plain south of the walls,
and the drums can be heard in Carholt, and then it stands down and goes home and does it
again the following spring.

Ambassadors have asked. Kings have asked. A Kadreshi envoy once asked directly at table,
and Lord Sarrec Tharryan is recorded to have said only: *"We are ready."* When the envoy
pressed him — ready for what — he is recorded to have repeated himself, word for word, and
then eaten his dinner.

**Words:** *We Are Ready*
**Banner:** A bronze spearhead upon black.

### The Castles of Sablereach

**HARDHOME** — *House Lossyne* — 80,000 souls, 25,000 swords
Cut directly into a lava shelf, with no well within its walls and no farmland within a day's
ride. By every principle of siegecraft it should be indefensible. It has never fallen. The
Lossynes have spent four centuries perfecting the art of needing very little for a very
long time, and the household still eats one meal a day by custom, in a castle that could
afford three.
**Words:** *We Need Little* · **Banner:** A grey wall upon red.

**SUNSTONE** — *House Ikar* — 70,000 souls, 17,000 swords
The gatehouse is faced in a single sheet of volcanic glass, and at dawn it throws the sun
back down the eastern approach so fiercely that an attacking column cannot look at it. This
was not designed. The first Ikar simply liked the stone. His great-grandson discovered its
use during a siege, by accident, and House Ikar have claimed divine favour ever since.
**Words:** *We Were Given Light* · **Banner:** A gold sun upon white.

**DRAGON CROSS** — *House Morren* — 58,000 souls, 4,900 swords
Four roads meet beneath a rock formation shaped, if one is generous, like a wing. The
Morrens tax all four and man almost nothing — fewer than five thousand swords for
fifty-eight thousand souls, the lightest garrison in Sablereach. They have calculated,
correctly, that no lord in the kingdom wants to be the one who closed the crossroads.
**Words:** *All Roads, One Toll* · **Banner:** Crossed roads upon grey.

**DRAGONHILL** — *House Drogo* — 50,000 souls, 24,000 swords
The royal seat of Sablereach, held by House Drogo, in a kingdom ruled by House Drago. The
two names differ by a single letter and the families differ by four centuries of a
disagreement neither will describe. Drago rules. Drogo holds the castle the ruling is done
from. Both houses insist, loudly and at every opportunity, that this is not a problem.
**Words:** *Fire Keeps* · **Banner:** A black wyrm upon red.

**CRASTERKEEP** — *House Craster* — 50,000 souls, 13,000 swords
The northern march of Sablereach, watching the Crownvale border. During the Long Winter
the Crasters opened their gates to a Crownvale column and fed them, and then closed the
gates behind them and held the column for the south. Both kingdoms have found the story
useful and neither has forgiven it.
**Words:** *We Face Both Ways* · **Banner:** A two-headed hound upon grey.

**SOUREANHALL** — *House Sourean* — 40,000 souls, 9,800 swords
Built above a sulphur spring. The halls smell of it, the linen smells of it, and the family
stopped noticing four generations ago. Guests are given rooms in the east wing and told the
wind usually carries it away. The Soureans live notoriously long lives and attribute this to
the water, which their maester has never disputed in writing.
**Words:** *Bitter Water, Long Life* · **Banner:** A yellow spring upon black.

**HIGHHORN** — *House Marrian* — 30,000 souls, 6,800 swords
The horn in its tower can be heard in three valleys. It has been sounded four times in six
hundred years, and each sounding is recorded on a brass plate below it. There is room on
the wall for a great many more plates. The Marrians have never explained whether this is
meant as a reassurance or a warning.
**Words:** *You Will Hear Us* · **Banner:** A bronze horn upon green.

**BEAR HOLD** — *House Rowe* — 25,000 souls, 6,000 swords
There has not been a bear in Sablereach for two hundred years. The Rowes keep the name, the
banner, and a stuffed specimen in the great hall that is visibly moth-eaten and visibly
irreplaceable. Guests who remark on this are not invited twice.
**Words:** *Ours Was First* · **Banner:** A black bear upon gold.

**HULLHOLD** — *House Hull* — 20,000 souls, 4,700 swords
A ship's hull sits in the courtyard, nine miles from the nearest water. A Sablereach king
had it dragged there to settle an argument about what his people could be made to do. The
argument is forgotten. The hull is still in the courtyard, and House Hull have maintained
it, at their own expense, for three hundred years.
**Words:** *We Carry Far* · **Banner:** An upturned hull upon blue.

**REDFIRE KEEP** — *House Redflame* — 20,000 souls, 5,000 swords
A vent below the keep glows on cold nights and has done for as long as anyone has written
things down. Pilgrims come to see it. House Redflame charge them, house them, feed them,
sell them a token, and are entirely unapologetic about the arrangement.
**Words:** *It Burns Below* · **Banner:** A red flame upon black.

**SABLEWATCH** — *House Tyland* — 8,000 souls, 2,000 swords
The smallest and southernmost holding in all Merovar. The Tylands watch the southern sea.
The charter that grants them the castle specifies what they are watching for, but that
portion of the document was water-damaged some centuries ago and no copy survives. The
Tylands watch anyway. They have never once asked the crown to clarify the terms.
**Words:** *Still Watching* · **Banner:** A black tower upon grey.

ago they were paid in land instead of coin after a campaign the crown could not afford, and
they took the land, and they never disbanded. They still muster by company. They still keep
the books.
**Words:** *Paid and Present*

## NORWOOD

### House Crowe · Seat at Highnest · 1,545,000 souls · 207,700 swords · 18 holdings

Ten castles. One city. That ratio is the whole character of the north.

Norwood is pine and cairn and iron, and it is the poorest of the three crowns of Merovar
by a considerable margin. It is also the only one that has never been conquered — not by
Crownvale, not by Sablereach, not by anything that has come down out of the unmapped
country beyond Northguard. The Norwood lords raise this in negotiations. They raise it at
feasts. They raise it, according to a Crownvale chronicler of some bitterness, "at
weddings, at funerals, and on at least one occasion during a fire."

The north does not build towns. It builds holds, and the people live near enough to run
for the gates.

---

### ELDERFELL — the City Among the Stones

**House Calder · 900,000 souls · 75,000 swords**

The standing stones came first. They are the oldest worked things in the world; nobody
knows who raised them, and the Kadreshi records — which cover everything — do not cover
these. The city grew up among them over centuries, streets bending around monoliths that
the builders would not move.

House Calder did not build Elderfell. They arrived, found it already old, and have spent
nine hundred years being extremely careful about how that sentence is phrased. Their words
are the finest piece of ambiguity in Merovar: *Older Than the Crown*. They do not say
older than *what* crown. They do not say the house is older. A Calder will let you assume
whichever you prefer and will correct nobody.

**Words:** *Older Than the Crown*
**Banner:** A grey standing stone upon white.

---

### The Castles of Norwood

**RAVENGUARD** — *House Demian* — 65,000 souls, 22,000 swords
The strongest fortress in the north, and more importantly the rookery of it. Every message
sent in Norwood passes through Ravenguard's birds, because the Demians bred the line and
train the keepers and no other house has ever managed to establish a rival loft that
survives a winter. This means, and everyone understands that it means, that House Demian
read the north's correspondence. They have never been accused of it. They have never denied
it either.
**Words:** *We Know First* · **Banner:** A black raven upon grey.

**LYKEHOLD** — *House Lyker* — 60,000 souls, 19,000 swords
A *lyke* is a corpse. The castle stands on a battlefield and was named by men who saw no
reason to be delicate. The Lykers keep a roll of every man known to have died on the ground
their walls stand on, and the reading of it takes four days, and it is read aloud in full
once every generation. Southerners find this morbid. The Lykers find southerners forgetful.
**Words:** *The Ground Remembers* · **Banner:** A white bone upon black.

**HIGHNEST** — *House Crowe* — 45,000 souls, 14,000 swords
The royal seat, on a crag with a single stair. Crowe kings are crowned at the top of that
stair, in the open, in whatever weather the day provides. Two have died of it. The custom
has not changed, and the family's position is that a king who cannot make the climb should
not be given the realm at the top of it.
**Words:** *We Perch Above* · **Banner:** A black crow upon white.

**WESTWATCH** — *House Mercer* — 36,000 souls, 9,000 swords
Six centuries of watching an empty western horizon. The Mercers maintain a signal chain
and man it in all weathers, and when asked what they expect to see, the answer given —
consistently, across generations, as though it were rehearsed — is *"something."* The
crown has twice proposed reducing the garrison. The Mercers have twice declined.
**Words:** *Something Comes* · **Banner:** A grey eye upon white.

**WOLFSDEN** — *House Grayskin* — 26,000 souls, 9,000 swords
The name refers to a hide, not a complexion. Which hide has been a matter of dispute for
three hundred years, since the family's own account and their neighbours' account differ
on a point that the neighbours consider important and the Grayskins consider settled.
**Words:** *We Wear What We Kill* · **Banner:** A grey wolf upon black.

**REDWATER KEEP** — *House Redmere* — 24,000 souls, 7,500 swords
The stream runs red from iron in the rock. Southern visitors assume otherwise, and the
Redmeres let them, because a reputation costs nothing to maintain and pays for itself the
first time a raiding party turns back at the ford.
**Words:** *It Is Only Iron* · **Banner:** A red stream upon grey.

**NORTHGUARD** — *House Norves* — 20,000 souls, 5,700 swords
The northernmost stone in the world. Beyond it the maps show a line, and past the line they
show nothing, because nothing that has gone past it has come back to be drawn. The Norves
hold the gate. They do not patrol beyond it. This is not cowardice and is not described as
such by anyone who has been there.
**Words:** *The Last Wall* · **Banner:** A white gate upon black.

**BLACKTOWER** — *House Buckley* — 14,000 souls, 3,200 swords
Genuinely black stone, from a seam that has never been found again despite four
generations of searching. When a portion of the west wall collapsed sixty years ago it was
rebuilt in grey, and the patch is visible from a league away, and the present Lord Buckley
has been trying to find a mason who can colour it convincingly ever since.
**Words:** *One of a Kind* · **Banner:** A black tower upon white.

**PINEGUARD** — *House Fell* — 11,000 souls, 2,500 swords
The great pine forest that roofs Norwood is Fell's charge. They permit one tree in twenty
to be cut, and they enforce it absolutely, and the enforcement has made them the least
popular and most necessary house in the north. Every hall in Norwood is timbered with wood
the Fells allowed out.
**Words:** *Ours Grows Slow* · **Banner:** A green pine upon white.

**FROZENFORT** — *House Ryne* — 9,000 souls, 3,000 swords
The moat has been solid ice for eleven years. After the first three the Rynes stopped
trying to thaw it, and after the first five they began holding a winter fair on it, and it
is now the best-attended gathering in the northern reaches. The castle is arguably more
defensible frozen, a point the family makes whenever the subject arises.
**Words:** *We Do Not Thaw* · **Banner:** A white star upon grey.

**Of Stonehill and House Hillard.** Eight hundred swords from thirty-three thousand souls —
one man in forty, the emptiest muster in the north by a wide margin, in the most warlike
kingdom on the continent. House Hillard are quarriers. Every fortress in Norwood, including
Highnest, including Ravenguard, including the walls of Elderfell, is built from Hillard
stone, cut by Hillard men, to Hillard measure.

They have never needed a garrison. It has never occurred to anyone to give them a reason
to want one.
**Words:** *We Cut What You Stand On*

# BOOK TWO: KADRESH

**6,305,000 souls · 1,215,000 swords · No crown · 13 holdings**

There is not one castle in Kadresh. Not a single fortified seat on the entire continent —
the surveyors checked twice, because the finding is difficult to credit and impossible to
explain to a Merovaran lord.

The east has walled cities, and between the walled cities it has desert, and in the desert
it has horsemen who own the water. That is the whole architecture of Kadreshi power. A
Crownvale general will tell you it is a fatal weakness, that a continent without strong
places is a continent that can be crossed. He will tell you this at length. He will not
mention that Kadresh has never been conquered, and that Merovar has tried three times, and
that on each occasion the army got as far as the second well and turned around.

Kadresh has no king. It has four great powers who despise each other, several lesser ones
who profit from the despising, and a horse-people who could end any of them by simply
staying home.

---

## THE KINGDOM OF QAZIRAH

**House Qasir · 1,550,000 souls · 300,000 swords**

The only true monarchy in the east and the largest single army on the continent. The Qasir
also hold **Palmwatch** and **the Silk Tower** — three of the thirteen holdings of Kadresh
in one family's hands, and a third of the eastern coastline.

They claim the sand itself. Not the cities on it, not the roads through it — the sand. It
is a peculiar claim to Merovaran ears, which understand ownership as a thing done to land
that grows something. The Qasir position, maintained across eleven centuries, is that
anyone can own a field, and that the true measure of a house is what it can hold when
there is nothing there to hold.

**Words:** *The Sand Was Ours First*
**Banner:** A gold palm upon white.

## THE EMPIRE OF LEEN

**Council of Seven · 1,500,000 souls · 210,000 swords**

An empire governed by committee, which sounds like a joke until one examines the accounts.

Seven merchant houses elect the Council. The Council has not reached a unanimous decision
in thirty-one years. Foreigners regard this as paralysis. The merchants of Leen regard it
as the system operating exactly as designed: a council that cannot agree cannot raise a
tariff, cannot seize a warehouse, cannot conscript a crew, and cannot declare a war that
would close the sea roads.

Leen has grown to a million and a half souls under a government that does nothing. Its
merchants consider this the strongest argument in political philosophy and make it
frequently.

**Words:** *Seven Voices, One Purse*
**Banner:** Seven silver coins in a ring upon deep blue.

---

## QORVEN CITY

**The Qor Prince · 1,100,000 souls · 140,000 swords**

One man rules Qorven absolutely. The title passes to whoever the dying prince names with
his last breath, and the naming is binding, and it is witnessed by seven men who are
executed if their accounts differ.

In eleven hundred years it has never once gone to a son. Not once. Princes have had sons —
capable ones, beloved ones, sons who commanded armies and were adored in the streets — and
at the end, every prince has named someone else. A general. A scribe. On one famous
occasion a stonemason, who ruled for forty years and is remembered as the best of them.

Nobody outside Qorven understands why. Inside Qorven it is not discussed, and a foreigner
who raises it at table will find the room has gone quiet and stays quiet.

**Words:** *Spoken, and So It Is*
**Banner:** A silver mouth, closed, upon purple.

---

## TRAVOS

**Council of Travox · 800,000 souls · 100,000 swords**

The oldest continuous government in the world. The same council, under the same charter,
in the same chamber, for eleven hundred years — through three fires, two plagues, and a
period of nineteen months when the chamber was under occupation and the council met in a
grain cellar and kept the minutes.

Those minutes still exist. All of them do. The Travox archive is the reason anyone knows
that Merovar was a Kadreshi colony, because Travos was writing things down when Crownford
was a fishing village, and Travos has never edited a page.

They regard the Empire of Leen, founded a mere six centuries ago, as an interesting recent
experiment.

**Words:** *We Were Voting Then*
**Banner:** An open ledger upon grey.

## THE KHARADA

**Vero Kharada — 320,000 souls, 165,000 riders**
**Vera Kharada — 250,000 souls, 130,000 riders**

Two settlements, one people, **295,000 horsemen** — one quarter of every soldier in
Kadresh, raised from a tenth of its population. One rider for every two souls, counting the
children and the old.

The cities call them raiders. It is a comfortable word and it is wrong. The Kharada do not
raid, because they have never needed to. They hold the wells. Every road between every city
in the east crosses water that Kharada riders water their horses at, and a city that gives
offence is not attacked, or besieged, or threatened.

It is simply made thirsty.

It has happened four times in recorded history. Each time the city sent an embassy within
the month, and each time the embassy walked, because the Kharada had also declined to sell
them horses.

**Words:** *We Hold the Water*
**Banner:** A white spring upon red.

## The Lesser Powers of the East

**MYRESH** — *the High Priest of Myresh* — 200,000 souls, 35,000 swords
The only theocracy on the map. The High Priest crowns no kings — Myresh has never had a
king and does not recognise the concept — but he buries them, all of them, from every
realm in Kadresh, because the burial rites of the east are Myreshi rites and always have
been. Every prince of Qorven and every king of Qazirah for eleven centuries lies in Myreshi
ground, attended by Myreshi priests, and this gives the city a form of leverage that no
army has ever been able to answer.
**Words:** *We Outlast Crowns* · **Banner:** A white flame upon deep blue.

**ZAMMETAR** — *the Kieen Clan* — 200,000 souls, 48,000 swords
Clan-held, not city-ruled. The Kieen are Kharada who dismounted four generations ago and
settled, and they are trusted by nobody: the cities regard them as horse-people playing at
civilisation, and the Kharada regard them as deserters. The Kieen have made a virtue of it.
They broker every agreement between the desert and the walls, and they charge both sides.
**Words:** *We Dismounted Once* · **Banner:** A broken stirrup upon sand.

**YEEN TI** — *the Qamar Clan* — 100,000 souls, 25,000 swords
Silk and dye. The Qamar hold the secret of a red that no dyer in the world has been able to
reproduce, and they have held it for three hundred years, and the method is known to four
people at any given time. Two of them are always travelling separately. One is always in the
city. The fourth is never named.
**Words:** *Ours Is the Deeper Red* · **Banner:** A crimson band upon black.

**YAN** — *House Yannesh* — 75,000 souls, 19,000 swords
The smallest and oldest port in the east, and the source of the most dangerous claim in
the world. House Yannesh assert that their ancestors captained the ships that carried the
first colonists west to Merovar. The Travox archive does not contradict them. If the claim
is true, then every noble house in Merovar — every Brenn, every Drago, every Crowe with
nine hundred years of pedigree on the wall — descends from Yannesh sailors.

No Yannesh envoy has ever been received at Crownford. The refusal is not explained. It has
never needed to be.
**Words:** *We Sent the Ships* · **Banner:** A silver prow upon green.

# BOOK THREE: AMBERFOLD

**3,291,000 souls · 295,500 swords · Two crowns · 26 holdings**

One sword for every eleven mouths, on the only ground in the world that feeds everyone
else. Every general who has ever looked at a map of Amberfold has had the same thought at
the same moment, and every one of them has then had the second thought, which is: *and
then what?*

Because taking Amberfold is easy and holding it is not. The fold has been invaded four
times. On each occasion the invader arrived, took the granaries, and discovered that a
granary is a promise rather than a thing — that the grain is grown by people who know
which fields are wet, which seed is stored where, and how to lose a harvest in a hundred
small ways that no occupying quartermaster can prove.

All four occupations ended in famine, and the famine was always worse for the occupier.

Amberfold's two crowns have meanwhile spent two hundred years arguing about water rights,
and would rather do that than anything else on offer.

---

## THE GREENMARCH

### House Liam · Seat at Golden Moot · 1,696,000 souls · 161,500 swords · 14 holdings

### GOLDEN MOOT — the Argued City

**House Liam · 1,200,000 souls · 80,000 swords**

A *moot* is a meeting. Not a throne, not a seat, not a hall — a meeting. The Greenmarch is
ruled from a place named for the act of disagreeing in public, and House Liam are less
kings than permanent chairmen of a conversation that has been going on for six hundred
years and shows no sign of concluding.

This is not weakness, though every visiting Merovaran lord decides it is within an hour of
arrival. A Liam king cannot command the Greenmarch. What he can do is refuse to close the
moot, and a lord who walks out of a session that has not been closed loses his standing to
speak at the next one, and a lord who cannot speak cannot object to what is decided while
he is absent.

Four rebellions have collapsed because the rebels came back to argue.

**Words:** *All May Speak*
**Banner:** A gold ring upon green.

---

### The Castles of the Greenmarch

**IRONMEED** — *House Harren* — 54,000 souls, 19,000 swords
One soldier in three, in a kingdom that raises one in eleven.

*Harren* is not an Amberfold name. It is hard where the fold is soft, northern where the
fold is warm, and it sits on the map like a stone in a loaf. The Harrens came from
somewhere colder, took this ground by force at a time the moot was distracted, and have
declined for two hundred years to soften the name to suit local manners. Every lord of the
Greenmarch must say *Harren of Ironmeed* aloud when treating with them, and every Harren
who has ever held the seat has enjoyed watching them do it.

They are the only house in Amberfold that took what it has. The rest of the fold has spent
two centuries agreeing not to find this relevant.
**Words:** *We Do Not Ask* · **Banner:** A black tower upon grey.

**SUMMERMARCH** — *House Quillian* — 39,000 souls, 12,000 swords
Every harvest tally in the Greenmarch is copied here and kept. The Quillians have never
lost a land dispute, not because they are favoured but because they can produce a document
and their opponent cannot. Three great houses have burned records in the last four
centuries. On each occasion the Quillians produced the duplicate they had quietly made,
and the burning house was ruined by the attempt rather than the claim.
**Words:** *Written, Therefore True* · **Banner:** A black quill upon gold.

**HIGHWALL** — *House Goldwyne* — 30,000 souls, 10,000 swords
The only genuine curtain wall in Amberfold, built by a family that made its fortune in
wine. Their neighbours mocked the expense for a generation. Then Harren came, and took
Ironmeed, and the mockery stopped in a single season, and three houses asked the Goldwynes
for the name of their mason.
**Words:** *We Built It Once* · **Banner:** A gold wall upon green.

**BRONZEHALL** — *House Wexley* — 28,000 souls, 10,000 swords
The oldest family in the fold, in a hall faced with bronze rather than stone — a statement
about age rather than wealth, since bronze was what the first builders had. The Wexleys
could have rebuilt in stone at any point in the last four hundred years. They have
declined, at some cost in comfort, because a stone hall would make them merely rich.
**Words:** *The Harvest Keeps* · **Banner:** A gold sheaf upon green.

**BLOODHAVEN** — *House Vexalia* — 25,000 souls, 9,000 swords
*Blood* here is lineage, and a *haven* is a refuge. It is a sanctuary seat — the place a
man goes when a feud is running and he needs neutral ground to stand on — and outsiders
have misheard it as something butcher's for four hundred years.

The Vexalias corrected people for the first century. They stopped. A reputation for
darkness costs a sanctuary nothing and deters exactly the sort of guest a sanctuary does
not want. Men who come to Bloodhaven in earnest learn the truth at the gate. Men who come
looking for the other thing do not come at all.
**Words:** *Come and Be Heard* · **Banner:** A red hand, open, upon cream.

**RYECASTLE** — *House Rye* — 10,000 souls, 2,500 swords
Rye grows where wheat will not — in thin soil, in sour ground, in cold that kills a wheat
crop outright. House Rye farm the worst land in the fold and are counted the poorest great
house in it, and in the last three famines they were the only house in the Greenmarch that
ate normally.

Their words sound like humility. They are not humility. In a good year *Bread Enough* is a
statement of modest means. In a bad year, standing in a hall where the golden houses have
come to ask, it is something else entirely.
**Words:** *Bread Enough* · **Banner:** Black rye-ears upon gold.

**Of Netherhythe and House Sedge.** A *hythe* is a landing place on a river, and *sedge* is

The present Lord Sedge is said to have remarked that he has been to precisely one wedding
outside his own lands in forty years, that the food was poor, and that he does not intend
to risk a second.
**Words:** *We Take the Low Water*

## AMBERLEY

### House Braxon · Seat at Kingsmeadow · 1,595,000 souls · 134,000 swords · 12 holdings

The only realm in the world with two cities of exactly equal size, which is presented in
every Amberley chronicle as a blessing and understood by every Amberley king as a problem.

**KINGSMEADOW** — *House Brax* — 600,000 souls, 35,000 swords
A meadow named for a king rather than a fortress named for a war. The Braxons hold the
crown of Amberley and rule from open ground, and the walls went up only three centuries
ago, and they went up reluctantly.
**Words:** *We Were Given This* · **Banner:** A gold crown upon green.

**CROWNS REST** — *House Tregane* — 600,000 souls, 25,000 swords
Precisely as large as the capital and ten thousand swords the weaker. This is not an
accident; it is the terms. Six hundred years ago the Treganes had the stronger claim and
declined to press it, and the settlement that followed fixed their strength below the
crown's in perpetuity.

They have never rebelled. Not once, through two contested successions and a regency that
invited it. And they have never quite been forgiven for being able to, because a house that
could take the throne and does not is a house the throne must think about every single day.
**Words:** *We Also Serve* · **Banner:** A silver crown, resting, upon russet.

### The Castles of Amberley

**ARRISHOLD** — *House Arrister* — 50,000 souls, 18,000 swords
The strongest castle in Amberfold, one sword in three, and the crown's own fist for six
generations. When a Braxon king needs a thing done that a king should not be seen doing,
the Arristers do it. They are not loved. They have never asked to be.
**Words:** *The King's Hand Closes* · **Banner:** A mailed fist upon green.

**SUNCREST** — *House Brightwell* — 50,000 souls, 12,000 swords
East-facing on the highest ridge in the kingdom, and it catches the dawn a full hour before
the valley below. The Brightwells hold court at first light by tradition, which means
petitioners must climb in darkness, which means only serious petitioners climb at all.
**Words:** *First Light Is Ours* · **Banner:** A gold sun rising upon white.

**GREENSTONE** — *House Jade* — 45,000 souls, 11,000 swords
Built from a green stone found in a single quarry, and the quarry is exhausted. The castle
cannot be repaired, only patched, and every patch is visible. House Jade have made a
philosophy of it: they do not restore, they do not disguise, and the great hall carries
four centuries of honest grey scarring on a green wall.
**Words:** *What Is Left Endures* · **Banner:** A green stone upon cream.

**GRAYHALL** — *House GrayLight* — 20,000 souls, 6,000 swords
Named for the light before dawn, and the family rides at that hour by custom — every
morning, in every season, whether or not there is anywhere to ride to. Three armies have
been surprised by GrayLight cavalry arriving at an hour when no sensible force is mounted.
The custom is now well known. It continues to work.
**Words:** *Before the Sun* · **Banner:** A grey lantern upon black.

**HIGHSPEAR** — *House Mervin* — 20,000 souls, 6,500 swords
A single tower, very tall and very thin, standing on flat open ground with no natural
advantage whatsoever. By every rule of fortification it should have fallen the first time
anyone tried. It has never been taken. The Mervins are asked to explain this constantly
and have never given the same answer twice.
**Words:** *One Point Is Enough* · **Banner:** A silver spear upon green.

The Merxes have survived four occupations of the fold by the simple expedient of not being

enjoyed the silence that followed.
**Words:** *Not Worth Taking*

# BOOK FOUR: THE ALDERMARCH

**815,000 souls · 101,900 swords · Two crowns · 17 holdings**

Six castles to one city in High Alden. Three castles to no city at all in Vennmark. The
Aldermarch does not build towns; it builds holds, and it argues about which hold outranks
which with a ferocity that no border dispute in the world has ever matched.

A Crownvale envoy who spent two seasons here wrote that the Aldermarch contains more
knights per acre than the rest of the world combined, that they are the finest heavy horse
he had ever seen, and that he had watched two of them nearly come to blows over who should
enter a doorway first. All three observations, he noted, were connected.

---

## THE KINGDOM OF HIGH ALDEN

### House Weyne · Seat at Weyne Port · 727,000 souls · 83,900 swords · 12 holdings

### WEYNE PORT — the Gate of the West

**House Weyne · 500,000 souls · 38,000 swords**

Two in every three Aldenmen live inside these walls. The kingdom is, functionally, one
great port and a ring of fortresses watching the roads that lead to it.

This concentration is deliberate and it is old. The first Weyne kings understood that a
cold coast with poor soil cannot support a scattered people, and that a scattered people
on a raided coast is simply a list of future casualties. So they gathered everyone in, and
they built the castles outward, and the Aldermarch became a kingdom shaped like a fist.

**Words:** *The Gate of the West*
**Banner:** A silver gate upon deep blue.

---

### The Castles of High Alden

**ASTERHOLD** — *House Allister* — 30,000 souls, 9,000 swords
A star-shaped hold, every wall angled so that no attacker can approach without being
enfiladed from two directions. The architect is not named in any surviving document, was
paid an enormous sum, and built nothing else anywhere. The Allisters have never permitted
the design to be surveyed and have refused four separate requests from the crown.
**Words:** *We Hold Every Angle* · **Banner:** A silver star upon black.

**CRIMSONKEEP** — *House Redboote* — 20,000 souls, 9,000 swords
Nearly one sword for every two souls — the most heavily armed holding in the Aldermarch,
and one of the most in the world. House Redboote have been at this readiness for as long
as the records run. Nobody now living knows what the first Redboote was preparing for. The
family maintain the muster regardless, on the grounds that their ancestor was not a fool
and knew something they do not.
**Words:** *Booted and Bloody* · **Banner:** A red boot upon white.

**BELLFORD** — *House Bell* — 19,000 souls, 6,000 swords
The bell is rung for war and for nothing else. It has sounded seven times in four hundred
years, and each ringing is carved into the tower wall with the year and the reason. There
is room for a great many more carvings. Visitors always ask about that. The Bells always
say the mason was optimistic, and change the subject.
**Words:** *You Will Hear It* · **Banner:** A bronze bell upon green.

**HIGHSTONE** — *House Ardren* — 18,000 souls, 7,000 swords
The highest hold in the kingdom, and the Ardrens do not descend from it. Not for summons,
not for coronations, not for funerals. Three kings of High Alden have made the climb to
Highstone to treat with an Ardren, and each has been received with perfect courtesy at the
top, and each has understood precisely what the courtesy meant.
**Words:** *We Do Not Kneel Twice* · **Banner:** A white hawk upon red.

**GREYGUARD** — *House Cain* — 6,000 souls, 1,000 swords
The weakest castle in High Alden by garrison and the one nobody has ever attacked. House
Cain hold the burial roads — every funeral procession in the kingdom passes their gate, and
every noble house in the Aldermarch has kin in ground the Cains keep. To take Greyguard
would be to take custody of every family's dead simultaneously, and no lord has ever wanted
that responsibility, or that grudge.
**Words:** *We Bury All* · **Banner:** A grey spade upon black.

**THE NEST** — *House Sparrow* — 5,000 souls, 900 swords
The smallest holding on the continent, on a crag reached by a rope stair that can be cut
from above in the time it takes to say so. House Sparrow have never lost it. No army has
ever thought the climb worth the trouble, which is the entire military doctrine of the
house, stated openly, and taught to Sparrow children as a virtue.
**Words:** *Small and High* · **Banner:** A brown sparrow upon white.

## VENNMARK

### House Venn · Seat at Cormaris · 88,000 souls · 18,000 swords · 5 holdings

The island kingdom — one great isle and two lesser. Eighty-eight thousand souls, which
makes it the smallest realm in the world by a margin of six hundred thousand, and the only
one with no city at all.

Three castles and two towns. It has outlasted kingdoms twenty times its size, and the
Venns are quietly certain they know why: an island cannot be marched on, only landed on,
and a landing is a thing you can watch coming for two days.

**CORMARIS** *(castle)* — *House Venn* — 20,000 souls, 6,000 swords
A house named Venn holding a seat named Vennhold would sound like a family that had run out
of ideas. *Cormaris* sounds inherited — older, softer, from somewhere else. The Venns took
the name from the cormorant colonies on the sea cliffs below, and they have never confirmed
this, because letting people assume the name is ancient costs nothing.
**Words:** *The Sea Keeps Us* · **Banner:** A silver cormorant upon green.

**UNDERGATE** *(castle)* — *House Corvane* — 10,000 souls, 2,000 swords
It sits below the pass rather than above it, which every visiting military man calls a
mistake. It is not. The Corvanes are not there to stop what comes down the road. They are
there to count it, name it, and send word ahead — and a castle that everyone has already
passed is a castle nobody thinks to search.
**Words:** *We Watch the Roads* · **Banner:** Three black rooks upon gold.

**RAYWATH** *(castle)* — *House Ray* — 9,000 souls, 3,000 swords
One sword in three, on an island that has not been invaded in living memory or in the
memory of anyone's grandfather. The Rays drill regardless. Asked why by a Crownvale guest,
the present lord is said to have answered that the reason Vennmark has not been invaded in
living memory is that people keep asking that question and receiving that answer.
**Words:** *Ready Regardless* · **Banner:** A silver ray upon blue.

**WINDFALL** *(town)* — *House Braylock* — 40,000 souls, 6,000 swords
Nearly half of Vennmark lives here. The Braylocks are gaolers by ancient right: every
oathbreaker in the Aldermarch, from either kingdom, is sent to Windfall, and the sending
houses pay for their keep. A *windfall* is something that drops into your hands without
being earned. The Braylocks did not choose the name. They have grown extremely comfortable
with it.
**Words:** *All Falls Here* · **Banner:** A black key upon grey.

**BELLSTOW** *(town)* — *House Roost* — 9,000 souls, 1,000 swords
A great house with a humble name, at the foot of the stair where every cart in Vennmark
must stop and every traveller must pass a night. House Roost are richer than either castle
above them and take considerable care that nobody notices. Their words are the driest joke
in the Aldermarch, and they are said to have been chosen at a feast where the great houses
were comparing the heights of their seats.
**Words:** *We Sit at the Bottom* · **Banner:** A brown hen upon cream.

# BOOK FIVE: THE SILVER ISLES

**House Peake · Seat at Salt Port · 513,000 souls · 81,800 swords · 16 holdings**

Rock, narrow channels, cliffs, and a shore the storms come at directly. There is almost no
farmland. There are deep harbours, hard timber, iron in the hills, and the sea roads of the
world passing directly overhead.

**On the name.** There is no silver in the Silver Isles. There never was, not an ounce, not
a seam, not a rumour with any substance to it. In winter the storm-light off the wet cliffs
turns the whole archipelago a hard silver-grey for weeks together, and that is where the
name comes from, and the first outsiders who heard it assumed it meant treasure.

They came. They found rock, weather, and a people who watched them look and did not offer
an opinion. They left.

The islanders have never corrected anyone, in four hundred years, and they are not going to
start. *Stoneborn* is what men call these islands once they have actually been to them.

**The Captain's Law.** Skill at sea outranks wealth and birth. A captain-lord holds his
seat only so long as he can still take a ship out; when he cannot, the harbour chooses
another — from his house if his house has produced someone worth choosing, and from outside
it if not. Sons inherit ships. They do not inherit titles.

Mainland lords assume this makes the isles unstable. What it means in practice is that a
silverborn commander has never in four centuries inherited his position from an incompetent
father, and that a very great many mainland commanders have.

---

### SALT PORT — the Great Yard

**House Peake · 200,000 souls · 20,000 swords**

Nine dry docks. The harbour chain has never been broken — not raised in vain, not cut, not
run. Every fleet in the known world was built here or built from a design that was.

The Peakes do not rule the isles; nobody does. What they hold is the yard, and the yard
holds the drawings, and no keel is laid anywhere in the archipelago to a plan the Salt Port
masters have not approved. It is not a law. It is simply that nobody has ever wanted to be
the captain who sailed a hull the yard had doubts about.

**Words:** *The Keel First*
**Banner:** A white keel-rib upon black.

---

### The Castles of the Isles

**TIDEWATCH** — *House Silverfin* — 29,000 souls, 7,000 swords
Reachable on foot twice a day and by no means at all the rest of the time. The Silverfins
time everything to the tide — councils, courtships, executions, and on two recorded
occasions a war. Their reckoning of hours is different from the mainland's and they have
never seen a reason to adopt anyone else's.
**Words:** *Twice a Day* · **Banner:** A silver fish upon blue.

**STONE HARBOUR** — *House Flint* — 24,000 souls, 6,500 swords
The deepest anchorage in the isles, capable of taking a hull that no other harbour will
float. The Flints have never turned a ship away in four hundred years and have never once
charged less than the absolute maximum the captain could pay. Both facts are advertised.
**Words:** *Struck and Sparking* · **Banner:** A grey flint upon black.

**SEAGUARD** — *House Vane* — 20,000 souls, 5,600 swords
Watching the strait toward Merovar. The Vanes have changed allegiance twice in their
history and admit to both changes freely, in detail, with dates. A house that lies about
turning may turn again in secret. A house that documents it is telling you the terms.
**Words:** *We Turn* · **Banner:** An iron weathervane upon slate.

**SILVERGATE** — *House Merrywyne* — 16,000 souls, 4,000 swords
The most cheerful name in the archipelago, belonging to its least cheerful family. The
Merrywynes are famously grim, famously humourless, and famously good at exactly one thing,
which is the accounting of cargo. Their words are the only joke they have ever been known
to make, and they made it four generations ago.
**Words:** *We Were Named Wrong* · **Banner:** A silver gate upon grey.

**STORMGUARD** — *House Darnell* — 15,000 souls, 5,700 swords
Facing open ocean with no shelter of any kind. The sea wall is rebuilt every generation as
a matter of course, and the rebuilding is a festival rather than a hardship, and Darnell
children are taught the courses of stone before they are taught letters.
**Words:** *We Have Weathered Worse* · **Banner:** A black cloud upon gold.

**COLDTIDE** — *House Frost* — 12,000 souls, 3,500 swords
The water here does not warm, not in summer, not ever. Frost divers are the finest in the
world — they salvage what no other crew can reach — and they are old at forty and rare at
fifty. Every Frost knows this before they take their first breath under. The house has
never had trouble finding volunteers.
**Words:** *Cold Keeps Longer* · **Banner:** A white wave upon grey.

**STORMGATE** — *House Blackwell* — 10,000 souls, 3,500 swords
A channel so narrow that two hulls cannot pass abreast. The Blackwells have held it against
fleets — actual fleets, more than once — for the simple reason that a fleet in that channel
is not a fleet. It is a queue.
**Words:** *One at a Time* · **Banner:** A black well upon white.

**NYKEFORD** — *House Nyke* — 8,000 souls, 3,000 swords
House and seat share a name, which in the isles marks the oldest claim there is. The Nykes
were here before the Captain's Law — before skill outranked blood — and they were the first
family of the archipelago under the old reckoning, and they are the smallest holding in it
under the new one.

They have never spoken against the Law. They have also never quite stopped mentioning what
they were before it.
**Words:** *We Were the First Crew* · **Banner:** A grey prow upon black.

# BOOK SIX: THE TATTERS

**77,000 souls · 46,500 swords · No crown · 6 holdings**

Three people in every five here carry a blade. There is no nation on earth that sustains
that ratio, because no nation needs to, and no nation could feed it.

The Tatters do not need to feed it. The Tatters feed it from other people's holds.

These are the scattered isles astride the only sea road that matters — the passage between
Merovar and Kadresh — and that position is the entire reason they have never been cleared.
Both great powers have run the numbers. Clearing the Tatters costs a fleet and a season.
Paying the Tatters costs a tithe on cargo. And clearing them has a second cost nobody in
Crownford or Qazirah says aloud, which is that the moment the isles are empty, Merovar and
Kadresh share a border.

There are no houses here. There are syndicates, clans, one republic, and a city council,
and the difference between them is mostly how recently they were founded and how seriously
they wish to be taken.

**BONEGUARD** *(castle)* — **the Saltblood Syndicate** — 25,000 souls, **22,000 swords**
Nine souls in every ten under arms. This is the most militarised place in the world and it
is not close; the second-place holding arms seven in ten and is a city twenty times the
size.

Saltblood are not pirates and become extremely difficult about the word. They are a navy —
organised in squadrons, ranked, provisioned, drilled — that has simply never sworn to a
crown. They take contracts. They have escorted more Merovaran cargo than they have ever
taken, a statistic they quote often and which is, inconveniently for their reputation,
true.
**Words:** *We Owe No Crown* · **Banner:** A white bone upon red.

**RED REEF** *(castle)* — **the Greyhook Republic** — 10,000 souls, 8,000 swords
The only republic on the map. Captains vote, and the vote binds — for exactly one voyage,
after which everything is open again. There is no standing government, no continuity, no
office that outlasts a single sailing.

Mainland scholars call it chaos. Greyhook captains point out that no Greyhook officer has
ever had time to become a tyrant, and that this is more than Qorven can say.
**Words:** *Until the Voyage Ends* · **Banner:** A grey hook upon green.

**STORMBREAK** *(castle)* — **the Red Eye Syndicate** — 8,000 souls, 6,500 swords
Named for the rock that splits the weather coming off the southern ocean. Red Eye keep the
sea in conditions that send every other crew to shelter, and they take ships whose captains
had reasoned, correctly by any normal standard, that nothing would be out in that.
**Words:** *We Sail the Worst of It* · **Banner:** A red eye upon black.

**DRIFTWOOD** *(town)* — **the Scarling Syndicate** — 13,000 souls, 4,000 swords
Built entirely from wreck timber. No two buildings match, no two roofs sit at the same
pitch, and the whole town is, in the strictest legal sense, stolen. The Scarlings maintain
that the sea gave it and the sea's title is the oldest there is, and no court that matters
has ever been asked to rule.
**Words:** *It Washed Up Ours* · **Banner:** Crossed spars upon grey.

**CRAB TOWN** *(town)* — **the Lowtide Clan** — 12,000 souls, 4,000 swords
The only holding in the Tatters that feeds itself. Lowtide farm the shallows — crab, weed,
salt, a little grain in the sheltered corner of the island — and they are mocked
relentlessly for it by every crew in the isles.

They are also the only people in the Tatters who have never gone hungry in a bad season,
and in three separate winters the mocking crews have come to buy from them at prices they
did not enjoy paying.
**Words:** *We Eat Regardless* · **Banner:** A red crab upon cream.

**TARPORT** *(town)* — **the Tarport City Council** — 9,000 souls, 2,000 swords
A city council. In the Tatters. With minutes, and a seal, and a written procedure for the
election of a harbourmaster.

Tarport has been going legitimate for three generations and has never quite arrived. The
council levies proper duties and issues proper receipts, and a substantial portion of what
passes through the harbour still cannot be described in the ledger without difficulty, and
the clerks have developed a formidable vocabulary for describing it anyway.
**Words:** *Duly Recorded* · **Banner:** An open ledger upon black.

# THE THREADS

*Being the matters a reader of this chronicle should keep in mind.*

**The southern arithmetic.** Sablereach fields 964,000 swords. Crownvale fields 309,900.
Norwood 207,700. The south could take Merovar in a season and has not tried in four
hundred years, and no king in Crownford has ever gone to sleep without knowing the numbers.

**Brinze Porn.** Nine hundred thousand people, six hundred and fifty thousand soldiers.
Seven in ten. That one city out-musters the Aldermarch, the Silver Isles and the whole of
Amberfold together. House Tharryan drill it every spring and stand it down every spring
and have said only, for six hundred years, that they are ready.

**The wells.** The Kharada raise 295,000 riders from 570,000 souls, and every road in
Kadresh crosses water they own. The four great cities of the east muster 750,000 between
them and cannot march a mile of it without Kharada consent.

**The undefended granary.** Amberfold raises one sword for every eleven mouths, on the only
ground in the world that feeds everyone else. House Harren of Ironmeed raise one in three,
under a northern name, on ground they took by force. The Greenmarch has agreed for two
hundred years not to find this relevant.

**No castles in the east.** Not one, on a continent of six million people. Walled cities
and horsemen and nothing between. Merovaran generals call it a fatal weakness and have
called it that through three failed invasions.

**The Yannesh claim.** House Yannesh of Yan — seventy-five thousand souls, the smallest
port in Kadresh — say their ancestors captained the ships that carried the colonists west.
The Travox archive does not contradict them.

No Yannesh envoy has ever been received at Crownford. The refusal has never been explained.
It has never had to be.
